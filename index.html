"I penny-pinch every joule, every gram, every second. Because out here, the math is the only thing that doesn't lie."

Estimate the local gravitational acceleration g using nothing but a string, a small weight, and a stopwatch — the same way humans figured out gravity centuries before they ever left Earth. Ryland Grace would approve.

SYSTEM STATUS: NOMINAL  •  CREW: AWAKE  •  OBJECTIVE: MEASURE g

PHASE 01

SETUP & PROCEDURE

// MATERIALS REQUIRED

➢ String / shoelace
⚿ Key, washer or small weight
✂ Tape
📏 Ruler / measuring tape
⏱ Stopwatch (provided below)
1

Build the pendulum

Tie the weight to one end of the string. Hang the other end from a fixed point — a shelf, a lamp, a curtain rod, or a broom balanced between two chairs. Aim for a length of about 1 meter (3 ft).

2

Measure the length L

Measure from the pivot point to the center of the weight. Enter this value in the next phase. Precision here matters most — it's squared in nothing, but it's linear in g.

3

Pull aside & release

Pull the weight a small angle to one side (under ~15°) and release gently — do not push. A small swing keeps the physics honest.

4

Time 10 full swings

Start the stopwatch and count 10 complete swings (over and back = 1 swing). Timing 10 instead of 1 divides your reaction-time error by ten. Record the total time, then repeat.

// CREW PREDICTIONS (discuss before you measure)

  • Does a heavier weight swing faster?
  • Does a bigger arc swing faster?
  • Does a longer string swing faster?

Most people get at least one wrong. The pendulum doesn't care what you believe.
